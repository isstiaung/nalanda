### Added
- **Formats and editions.** Every item can say which forms it is held in — hardcover, paperback, ebook, audiobook; LP, 7", 10", CD, cassette; boxed, expansion, print-and-play; digital — as **Held as** checkboxes on its form, pills beside its type on its page and on share pages, and a **Format** filter on every shelf. A book added from Open Library and a record from Discogs come with the form the provider named. **Also held as**, on the item form, lists the other editions you hold or have scanned — format, ISBN or barcode, publisher, year — so scanning the audiobook's barcode finds the paperback's item ("In your catalog") instead of adding it again; it stays inside the app, like the ISBN. When an item is held in more than one form, **Lend** asks which copy is going out, and the loan says so on the item page and the Loans page. One item per work: reads, reviews, series and the cover stay the work's. Both round-trip through the CSV (`formats`, `editions`, and `edition` in the loans cell).
- **Language and original title.** An admin sets the household's language under **Members → Household language** (any language; English to begin with). Every item added takes it unless its source says otherwise — Open Library and Google Books name a book's language, and a Nalanda export carries it — and any item's language can be changed on its form. An item whose language differs from the household's shows it as a pill beside its type, so a shelf of one language stays plain. **Original title**, on the item form, is the title a work was first published under, in any script, shown under the title and found by search exactly as written (no transliteration). Both appear on share pages and go to connected households, like the publisher, and both round-trip through the CSV (`language`, `original_title`).
- **New from your authors.** Under Reading, a page lists the authors of the books you have finished, most first. **Look up** asks Open Library for an author's works, newest first — one request, only when you click — and shows them as Add-page results: what is already on your shelves is marked, the rest can go on a shelf, be logged as read, or join your want list. That, with a series' missing volumes, is the extent of what Nalanda suggests: no recommendation engine, and nothing about your reading sent anywhere.
- **Find a series' missing volumes.** A series' page gains **Find the missing volumes on Open Library**: one request, when you click, lists the works Open Library places in the series, and the ones with a number you're missing are offered with this series' name and that number filled in — add, log, or want. What you already hold, by number, ISBN or title, isn't offered again; works whose number Open Library doesn't know are listed apart. Your own series name, numbers and total are never changed. Open Library knows series for some works and not others, so an empty answer means only that.
- **Search operators.** The search box takes `author:`, `title:`, `tag:`, `status:` (unread, reading, read, abandoned), `year:` (2019, or 2010-2019), `lang:` (a code or a name) and `type:` (book, game, record) beside plain words — `author:"le guin" status:unread`, quotes for a phrase. They narrow the same search, so the best 50 are still the best 50 of what you asked; operators on their own list by title. A prefix the box doesn't know, or a value it can't read, is searched as the text it is. A line under the box lists them.
- **A trash.** Deleting an item — from its page, or several at once — now keeps it for 30 days with everything it had: tags, series, reads, reviews, the pages recorded, plays, loans, want-list entries, purchase links and its cover. **Trash**, in the sidebar's Settings section for admins, lists what was deleted, by whom and when, with **Restore** and **Delete for good**. A restored item comes back on its shelf under a new number; a connected household sees it as newly added. After 30 days the trash lets it go, whether or not anyone opens the page. Who deleted it, and whose reads and reviews come back, are matched to members as they were then, never to someone given the same account number since. The delete itself is unchanged: a trashed item is off every shelf, share link, connection view, search and export the moment it's deleted.
- **A cover from your camera.** Under any item's cover — book, record or board game — **Add a cover from a photo** (or **Replace the cover with a photo**) takes a picture with the phone, or picks a file, and **Use this photo** makes it the cover; **Remove cover** sits beside it. The item form has the same field, for an add or an edit, and a photo there wins over a cover URL typed beside it. The browser shrinks the picture before it's sent (to 1,200 px on its long side, as a JPEG), so a phone's photo goes up in a second; the server keeps only real JPEG, PNG, GIF, WebP or AVIF files, up to 4 MB, and reads the type from the file itself. A photo is shown wherever the cover is — share pages included — like any other cover.
- **Share links preview where you paste them.** A share link dropped into WhatsApp, iMessage, Signal or anywhere that draws link cards now shows what it is: a shelf's name, how many items, and its first cover; an item's title, creators, type and the start of its description, with its cover; a gift list under the title its page has. The tags carry only what the page already shows — never notes, where things are kept, loans, prices or names the switches keep off — and share pages stay out of search engines as before.
- **Creators and publishers have pages.** **Creators** in the sidebar's Library section lists everyone the catalog names — authors of books, designers of board games, artists of records, grouped by which they mostly are, with how many of each — and a box to narrow the list. Each name's page shows their items as a shelf does, sixty a page, headed by what they are and how many of their books you've finished. **Publishers** does the same for publishers and, for records, labels. A name is read from the item's Creators or Publisher field as it is, so "Le Guin, Ursula K." and "Ursula K. Le Guin" are one author, and "Terry Pratchett, Neil Gaiman" two. On an item's page, each creator and the publisher now link to their page. Inside the app only: share pages are unchanged.
- **Sign out other devices.** Account has a **Devices** card with one button: every other device signed in to your account is signed out at once, and the one you pressed it on stays in. A phone that went missing, a browser left signed in somewhere: no admin needed. Changing your password now does the same, and an admin's **Reset password** signs that member out everywhere as well as handing them a temporary password — so a reset is the remedy for someone else's lost phone. Nobody else's sessions are touched, and nothing needs a deploy or a secret.

### Changed
- The reading goal's note under the bar now reads "The mark shows where you'd be reading evenly since 1 January."
- **A record's cover now comes from the Cover Art Archive, never from Discogs.** Discogs' API terms restrict its images, and a stored cover is shown on share pages and to connected households. Adding a record from a Discogs result (search, barcode or a held scan), the cover backfill, and wanting a recommended record now look the record up on MusicBrainz, by barcode or by a confident artist-and-title match, and store the archive's front cover, or none. Discogs still fills in the pressing. The Add page still previews Discogs' image while you choose. A Discogs image URL pasted into the cover field is refused, with the reason.
- **Pages read far less of the database.** The Overview reads about a fifth of the rows it did, a shelf about a sixth, a tag's page about a third and a tag's share link a quarter, and Year in review about half — with fewer database calls, and nothing on any page changes. On a 2,000-item catalogue a day's ordinary use comes to about 3% of the free plan's daily rows, down from 12%. The analysis is in [docs/perf/query-analysis.md](../docs/perf/query-analysis.md).

### Fixed
- **Today is the day where you are, not the server's.** Nalanda's server keeps UTC time, so every date it offered — on **Finish**, **Played** and **Start reading**, the day a first page opened a read on, the day a loan was made or returned, whether a loan was overdue, where a reading goal's pace stood, which year was "this year" — was UTC's day: a member in Chennai finishing a book at 2 am had it dated yesterday. The browser now tells the server its time zone, in a small `tz` cookie it keeps for a year, and every one of those dates is today there. A device with no zone to give, or whose first page loads before the cookie is written, gets UTC's day as before. Dates you type are unchanged, and so are the times shown for when something was added or recorded, which stay UTC.
- Date fields sat a pixel above the button beside them in Chrome at about half of row positions; they now sit level. Safari is unchanged.
- **Scans held offline can go straight onto your want list** from the Add page's review list (**Want to read** / **Want**), as search results can. A held scan of a book already in the catalog is wanted on that copy.
- The accessibility audit now presses the Add page's **More results**, and `npm run a11y -- --only=<word>` also picks out single htmx steps.

### Upgrading
- **One migration, 0043: a new `editions` table and two columns with defaults, no data changed.** [Back up](../runbooks/backup-and-restore.md) first as before any migration, then deploy as usual. Connections on older versions drop the new `formats` key and are otherwise unaffected.
- **Two migrations, 0044 and 0045: two columns and a setting with defaults, then the search index rebuilt to include the original title** (as 0032 did for location). No data changes; the rebuild reads every item once. [Back up](../runbooks/backup-and-restore.md) first as before any migration, then deploy as usual. Items from before the column read as the household's language. Connections on older versions drop the new keys and are otherwise unaffected.
- **One migration, 0042: a new `trash` table, no data changed.** [Back up](../runbooks/backup-and-restore.md) first as before any migration, then deploy as usual. Backups from now on include the trash. Connections are unaffected.
- **One migration, 0041: a column with a default, no data changed.** It adds `session_generation` to `users`, 0 for everyone. [Back up](../runbooks/backup-and-restore.md) first as before any migration, then deploy as usual. **Nobody is signed out by the upgrade**: every existing session cookie still works until its account changes its password, is reset, or presses Sign out other devices. Connections are unaffected.
- **Today in your own time zone** needs nothing: no migration, no secret, no setting. The first page each device loads after the upgrade still offers UTC's day; from the next page on it offers the device's. Connections are unaffected: nothing sent to or received from another household changes.
- **Replace record covers stored from Discogs** with the one-off in [record-covers.md](../runbooks/record-covers.md), soon after deploying: `npm run record-covers:remote -- rehearse --backup backups/remote-<date>`, then `export`, `enrich`, `upload`, `npm run backup`, `apply`. It touches only covers the data proves came from Discogs: a record added from a Discogs result and never saved since. It replaces each with the Cover Art Archive's, or drops it. Covers typed in by hand, and covers it can't place, are kept and counted. No migration, and no new secret. Connections on older versions are unaffected. A cover they copied from an old key shows its placeholder once that object is deleted.
- **One migration, 0040: indexes only.** It adds seven indexes and changes no data. [Back up](../runbooks/backup-and-restore.md) first as before any migration, then deploy as usual — the deploy applies it. On a 2,000-item catalogue building them writes about 10,000 rows once, of the free plan's 100,000 a day; afterwards adding an item writes about four more rows than before.
- **Connections** are unaffected: nothing sent to or received from another household changes.
