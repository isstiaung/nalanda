### Added
- **Two contributor guides.** [docs/adding-a-column.md](../docs/adding-a-column.md) and [docs/adding-a-provider.md](../docs/adding-a-provider.md) walk the two most common changes in order — the existing rules, sequenced — linked from CONTRIBUTING.md. Nothing in the app changes.
- **Borrowed from someone.** A book borrowed from a friend is in the catalog as Not owned, with a borrow record: on its page, under Circulation, **Borrowed from**, a contact, a due date and a note, and **Mark returned** when it goes back. A **Borrowed** pill shows beside Not owned on the item, on shelves and in search; the shelf's Holding filter gains **Borrowed from someone**. **Borrowed**, under Lending, is now for every household — what is borrowed from people, overdue flagged, and what was returned — with the connections sections following where connections are enabled. Private like loans: never on a share link, never to connections. Round-trips through the CSV as a `borrowed` cell, shaped like `loans`.
- **Author A–Z.** A shelf's sort gains the author's surname — the first creator's last name, or the name before the comma when a creator is written "Le Guin, Ursula K." — then their full name, then the title; items with nobody named come last. A share link can be published in that order too.
- **Import from StoryGraph and LibraryThing.** Both exports are recognised on the Import page and brought in as a Goodreads export is: a book already here (by ISBN, then title and author) gets your rating, review and reads merged onto it; the rest become Not owned reading-log entries — or owned copies where the file says so (StoryGraph's Owned?, LibraryThing's collections and Copies). StoryGraph's dated reads each become a read, its format the copy's form, its moods, pace and content warnings your private notes; LibraryThing's Date Read and Date Started, Media, Comment and Private Comment, Collections, Series and Volume, Languages all map. Two runbooks say how to export.
- **QR codes for share links.** Shared links shows a QR code beside each address — lampblack modules on palm-leaf, the Nalanda mark under its vermilion rule in the middle — with **Download PNG** for a card on the shelf or a note on the fridge. Drawn in your browser from the address already on the page; nothing new is published, and nothing is fetched from anywhere else.
- **Item history, for admins.** An item's page gains **History**: each change to one of its own fields — title, creators, shelf, holding, cover, notes, location, series, price, details — with who made it and when, from every path that changes an item (the form, the Holding toggle, bulk edit, a cover, a refresh from BGG or Discogs, an import), kept 90 days. Not reads, reviews or plays, which already say who. A removed member is "a former member". Members don't see it; nothing of it reaches share links or connections.
- **A read-only API, with tokens of your own.** **Account → API tokens** makes a token, shown once; with it a script or another app reads your library as JSON — `/api/v1/items` with a shelf's filters, 250 a page; an item with its reads, reviews and loans; search; loans; your want list and goal — and changes nothing. A token sees what you see and no more; it stops working when you sign out other devices or change your password, and you can revoke it any time. [runbooks/api.md](../runbooks/api.md).
- **Feeds for share links.** Every share link has an Atom and an RSS feed — `/share/<token>/feed.atom` and `.rss`, linked from the page's head so a reader finds them — of its twenty newest additions (a gift list's newest wants), each with the title, creators, cover, the household's rating and latest review, and a link to the item's share page. The same whitelist as the page; dated by when the item was added, never by anyone's reading. Cached with the page, gone with the token.
- **Date added, from the file.** A Goodreads, StoryGraph or LibraryThing import dates each new book by the file's Date Added or Entry Date (libib's `added` likewise) instead of the day of the import, so newest-first and "Unread for years" mean what they say. For the books already here, tick **Also set the date added of books already here from the file** on the Import page and re-import the same export: the preview says how many it would date, the summary how many it did. Off by default; nothing else about a book moves, and connections keep every reference they hold.

### Fixed
- **The login throttle now holds against guesses sent all at once, and counts per account as well as per address.** Ten wrong passwords in ten minutes from one address already locked it out, but a burst sent in parallel all reached the password check before any was counted; the attempt is now counted in the statement that checks the count, before the password is looked at, so a burst stops at ten too. The rule has changed in two ways: ten wrong guesses at one account, from however many addresses, lock that account out for ten minutes (the right password included — before, only the guessing address was slowed), and a household's shared address locks out only the accounts guessed at on it, not everyone behind it. The lockout page reads as it did and now answers HTTP 429, so a script can tell it from a wrong password. A login that succeeds counts towards nobody's ten.
- **Login no longer says, by how long it takes, which usernames exist.** A name nobody has was answered without checking a password, in a fraction of the time a wrong password took; it is now checked against a fixed stand-in hash, so both answers take the same work.
- **Guessing at your current password under Account is throttled like login.** Changing a password asks for the current one, and that check had no limit: whoever held a signed-in device could try passwords without end, and a right guess would have signed you out everywhere. Wrong answers there now count towards the same ten in ten minutes, by address and by account, and past it the page says "Too many attempts" (HTTP 429) — the account's login is locked out for those minutes too.
- **A temporary password reaches only the password change.** Signed in with a temporary password, a member could already open nothing but Account — but every action on that page still went through: a display name (which share pages show when names are on), **Sign out other devices**, revoking a token. Until their own password is set, those are sent back to the Account page, unchanged; logout works as before.
- **Members no longer offers "Reset password" on your own row.** A reset signs its account out everywhere — the device that clicked included — so an admin who reset themselves was signed out on their next click, with the temporary password shown once on a page about to go. Your own row now points at **Account**, where changing your password keeps this device signed in; a reset posted at your own account anyway is refused.
- **Signed-in pages are no longer kept by the browser.** On a shared phone, one person's logout followed by another's Back could show the first person's pages from the browser's own cache — notes, locations, who has what on loan, a temporary password just minted. Every signed-in page now tells the browser not to keep it (`Cache-Control: no-store`), and logging out clears the browser's cache of this site. Share links, covers and the login page are unchanged.
- **Static files carry the same protective headers as pages.** The offline page, scripts, stylesheet and icons are served by Cloudflare before Nalanda runs, so they went out without the headers every page has; a `_headers` file in `public/` now marks each one unframeable (`X-Frame-Options: SAMEORIGIN`) and `nosniff`. Nothing else about them changes: they still revalidate on every load.
- **A NUL byte in the search box answers a page, not "Something went wrong".** The full-text index read the byte as the end of its expression; it is now dropped, with the other control characters, and the words either side of it still search.
- **An operator beside a stray dash, ampersand or ellipsis lists what the operator lists.** `tag:fantasy &` and `status:unread -` found nothing where `tag:fantasy` and `status:unread` listed; a word with no letter or digit in it is now no word. `pratchett & gaiman` finds Good Omens as it did.
- **A saved view holds "Read by" as the menu writes it.** A view saved from a hand-typed `readBy=02` named member 2 and yet kept that filter when they were removed, so it would have listed whoever was given the id next under the old name. The id is stored without leading zeros, views saved before this are matched as numbers when a member goes, and the search box's menu shows such a value selected.
- **"On loan" and "Overdue" count every open loan.** The Overview and the Loans page counted a list that stops at the newest 200, so past that both numbers stuck at 200 and the oldest loans — the likeliest overdue — went uncounted. Both are now counted in the database; the Loans table still lists the newest 200 and says so when there are more.
- **New from your authors and a series' look-up read the shelves once.** The Discover page listed the shelves twice on every load, and a series' Open Library look-up read the series twice and the shelves twice; each now reads what it shows once, which leaves more of the day's database budget for everything else.

### Upgrading
- **One migration, 0053: a new column on `login_attempts` (`username`), no data changed.** [Back up](../runbooks/backup-and-restore.md) first as before any migration, then deploy as usual. The table is throttling state, left out of backups and stale within minutes; nothing in it survives the hour.
- **One migration, 0051: a new `api_tokens` table, no data changed.** [Back up](../runbooks/backup-and-restore.md) first as before any migration, then deploy as usual. The backup's table order gains `api_tokens`.
- **One migration, 0052: a new column on `items` (`created_at`, empty until an import re-dates a book), no data changed.** [Back up](../runbooks/backup-and-restore.md) first as before any migration, then deploy as usual. Then, to date a catalogue imported from Goodreads before this version, re-import the same export with the box ticked ([runbook](../runbooks/import-from-goodreads.md#dates-added)).
- **Two migrations, 0049 and 0050: two new tables (`item_history`, and the `acting` marker, empty between writes) and one trigger on `items`, no data changed.** [Back up](../runbooks/backup-and-restore.md) first as before any migration, then deploy as usual. History starts at the upgrade; nothing is backfilled, and every item write sweeps what is older than 90 days. The backup's table order gains both tables.
- **One migration, 0048: a new `borrows` table, no data changed.** [Back up](../runbooks/backup-and-restore.md) first as before any migration, then deploy as usual. The backup's table order gains `borrows`.
